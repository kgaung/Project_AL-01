Origin

A fish with a titanium heart

Most people look up when they think about exploration. Rockets. Space stations. Moons and Mars. But on a calm night in the North Pacific, our most advanced explorer didn't leave the planet — it slipped quietly downward, into 6,000 meters of water and darkness.

When surface nations accelerated space programs and left ocean science chronically under-funded, a private consortium formed the Abyssal Initiative to finish exploring our own planet first. KANEDEV Marine Systems won the flagship contract: design a vehicle that could dive as deep as the great trenches, disturb the environment as little as possible, and operate for days without surface control.

Instead of another torpedo-shaped sub, KANEDEV's engineers borrowed from tuna, snailfish, and manta-ray physiology to create a fish-shaped hull that could swim rather than shove water aside with propellers. The goal was stealth, efficiency, and precise maneuvering around fragile vent fields and coral structures.

"We didn't want to build a machine that goes deep. We wanted to build one that belongs there."

Hull Construction
Inside the streamlined body sits a 2.1-metre titanium sphere — a Ti-6Al-4V pressure hull with ~75–80 mm walls, rated for repeated dives to 6,000 m with a design test margin to 8,000 m. The outer composite shell handles hydrodynamics. Syntactic foam fills remaining volume for buoyancy at hadal depths.

Intelligence

The onboard scientist AI

To support long, risky missions, the team embedded an AI "researcher pilot" inside the vehicle — software that interprets sonar, video, and chemistry data, detects anomalies, and re-plans missions on the fly. Human operators log in as mission commanders rather than joystick pilots, while the sub chooses transects, sampling points, and when to "sit still and listen."

  • Acoustic & optical comms to surface
Mission Log

41.5 hours in the deep

T + 0 h
Launch from RV Capryos
AL-01 released above Shinkai Rift. Controlled descent begins using trim ballast and minimal tail oscillation. Mission intent uploaded — no waypoints, only goals.
T + 3.6 h
Reached working depth — 5,200 m
Autonomy core transitions to survey mode. Sonar dome activates. Ladder-pattern transect begins at 50–70 m standoff from the rift wall.
T + 11 h
Anomaly Detected — Methane Seep Field
Science layer flags high backscatter + methane spike. Confidence: probable cold seep field. AI self-retasks — drops to 1 kn, initiates micro-survey. Surface team approves via acoustic link.
T + 18–22 h
Sampling Operations
AL-01 hovers 3–4 m above the seafloor using pectoral fin micro-corrections. Niskin water samples taken across plume. Sediment cores extracted near chemosynthetic patches.
T + 41.5 h
Surface Recovery
Battery reserve hits 35% threshold — AI initiates return automatically. Efficient cruise gait engaged. Compressed science previews transmitted. AL-01 surfaces on schedule.

By hour 10, AL-01 had already mapped more of the Shinkai Rift segment than all previous cruises combined. Tail-driven propulsion produced smooth, low-jitter motion — sharper sonar strips and less backscatter than thruster-only designs.

The pale mats and clumped invertebrates on camera told the full story: a chemosynthetic community feeding on methane seeping out of the sediment. A new site, undescribed by science. And AL-01 found it — entirely on its own, without a single joystick override.
